# Detection Quality

Measured over **50 simulated days** (seeds 0 to 49) of
varied attacks, evasions and benign look-alikes. Generated by
`python -m soclab.detections.evaluation --seeds 50`.

## False positive causes

- none

Benign scenarios are handled correctly when nothing alerts on them; malicious ones
when every rule meant to catch them fires.
## Reading these numbers

- **Precision 1.00** means no false alarms on the benign activity that is simulated: staff
  typos, internet background noise, and a backup job retrying an expired password. That job
  first gave `ssh_bruteforce` a precision of 0.61; a scoped exclusion (that user, from that
  subnet only) fixed it without losing a single detection. Real networks contain benign
  behaviour this lab does not simulate, so real-world precision will be lower.
- **`port_scan` recall 0.88:** every scan of 20 or more ports is caught; the misses are small
  scans of 11 to 14 ports. Lowering the threshold would start alerting on internet noise.
- **`ssh_bruteforce` recall 0.75:** every fast brute force is caught; every miss is the
  low-and-slow evasion (one guess a minute), which never reaches 10 failures in 5 minutes.
  Closing it needs a second, long-window rule.
- **Time to detect** is from the first attack event to the alert. Sprays are slow by design
  (10 distinct users needed, attackers pace themselves), so the median is about 9 minutes.
- **Alert grouping 1.16 alerts per incident:** in this simulation every attack comes from its
  own IP, so the only merges are a brute force plus its compromise alert. Real attackers reuse
  infrastructure across stages (scan, then guess, then log in), where grouping saves more.
